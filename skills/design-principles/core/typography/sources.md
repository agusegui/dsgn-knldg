## Type Classifications Are Useful, But the Common Ones Are Not — Indra Kupferschmid
- URL: https://kupferschrift.de/cms/2012/03/type-classifications/
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: high — academic/practitioner author who has taught and researched classification for 14+ years; based on Gerrit Noordzij's foundational writing theory
- Key extracts:
  - Three form models cut across the serif/sans distinction: Dynamic (broad-nib pen), Rational (pointed pen or constructed), Geometric (drawn/monoline)
  - "All fonts that stand in one vertical column combine well and harmoniously, whereas mixing horizontal neighbors is more tricky. For contrasting combinations you can pair diagonally."
  - The pairing rule: "Either stay in one form model or go for lots of difference."
  - Atmosphere mapping: Dynamic = warm, open, friendly; Rational = regular, strict, formal; Geometric = constructed, precise
  - Transitional Serifs sit between Dynamic and Rational — which explains their pairing versatility
  - "A typeface can only be 'one of those things' [is] not realistic" — typefaces live in fraying clusters, not discrete buckets
  - Classification has three purposes: sorting (scholars), referencing (education), finding (users) — most systems optimize for the first two and fail the third
- Pairings extracted: none (framework article, not pairing-specific)

## How to Use Clashing Fonts — Jonathan Hoefler / Hoefler&Co.
- URL: https://hoeflerco.com/journal/how-to-use-clashing-fonts
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: high — same author as Doubletakes; focused specifically on same-classification and near-identical pairings
- Key extracts:
  - "Make each font's purpose clear, and use every one consistently" — replaces the blanket "don't use similar fonts" rule
  - Related fonts can work together if each inhabits its own altitude OR its own semantic function
  - Optical size naming: "Fine/Display/Titling" = large sizes; "Text/ScreenSmart" = small sizes
  - "Routinely pairing one font's roman with the other's italic... in both directions" adds differentiation to close pairs
  - When combining same-source revivals, look to extreme weights/widths first — that's where the families diverge most
- Pairings extracted (H&FJ proprietary, not added to pairings.json): Quarto + Mercury ScreenSmart, Ideal Sans + Operator, Champion Gothic + Knockout

## Typographic Doubletakes — Jonathan Hoefler / Hoefler&Co.
- URL: https://hoeflerco.com/journal/typographic-doubletakes (article by Jonathan Hoefler)
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: high — written by a leading type designer; unusually specific about the mechanics of why pairings work
- Key extracts:
  - "Changes in both weight and scale help to accentuate the differences between these pairs, and draw out the unique character of each design."
  - "Typefaces don't need to share a common weight to be good comrades, especially if they're used at different sizes."
  - "Careful thought has been given to the tracking of each typeface, with the intention of synchronizing the rhythm of letters and the spaces between them."
  - "Keeping eccentric typefaces at arm's length helps accentuate their uniqueness... each typeface is assigned a specific range of sizes that's free of other fonts."
  - Even very different fonts can cohere if they share one motif — an angle of incline, a level of geometry, an era.
- Pairings extracted (all H&FJ proprietary, not added to pairings.json): Sentinel + Archer, Gotham + Verlag, Mercury Text + Ideal Sans, Vitesse + Gotham, Chronicle Hairline + Landmark

## Size-specific Adjustments to Type Designs (book review) — Typographica / Stephen Coles
- URL: https://typographica.org/books/size-specific-adjustments-to-type-designs/
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: medium — review of an academic book, not a how-to; practical yield is low but the core concept (optical sizing) is important
- Key extracts:
  - "Fonts made for small text looked clunky and inelegant when enlarged. Fonts made for headlines became anemic and unreadable when reduced for body."
  - Digital type lost size-specific optimizations when type went from metal to screen — optical size variants restore them.
- Pairings extracted: none
- Rule extracted: use Display/Text/Caption cuts at their intended sizes; treat optical size variants as a required pairing decision, not a style choice

## Upping Your Type Game — Jessica Hische
- URL: https://jessicahische.is/talkingtype
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: high — practitioner-level advice from a leading lettering artist and type user; written for web designers, unusually clear on process
- Key extracts:
  - "Figure out what kind of content is most prevalent in your project and choose the typeface that satisfies the needs of that content. This first typeface is your anchor."
  - "I'm not satisfied with the standard three weight set of light, regular, and bold. I like to work with typefaces that have inbetweener weights."
  - "There's a difference between a sloped roman and an italic... true italics ensure even color within text when used with their roman counterpart."
  - "One reason Helvetica doesn't work well for text is that its letter-spacing is just too damn tight."
  - The Il1 test: capital I + lowercase l + number 1 — if indistinguishable, legibility problem at text sizes.
  - Skeleton / Meat / Clothes framework: shared skeleton (x-height, width, proportions) is the structural basis for compatible pairing.
  - Sibling / Cousin / Distant Relative pairing spectrum — useful for gauging risk vs. reward.
- Pairings extracted: Freight Display + Freight Text + Freight Sans (superfamily example, paid)

## 3 Steps to Great Typeface Pairs — type-ed.com
- URL: https://type-ed.com/resources/rag-right/2017/10/18/3-steps-to-great-typeface-pairs
- Date accessed: 2026-03-07
- Quality: medium — practical framework from a type education platform, strong on process but light on specific examples and typesetting detail
- Key extracts:
  - "Type classifications fall into two basic categories: serif and sans serif" — with sub-classifications: humanist, transitional, modern, slab (serifs) and humanist, gothic, grotesque, geometric (sans)
  - X-height variation means fonts at identical point sizes appear different in size — must be accounted for when pairing
  - Allographs (single vs double-story 'a' and 'g') signal personality and tradition; mismatched allograph styles without intent feel unresolved
  - Axis orientation (diagonal humanist vs vertical geometric/modern) affects whether paired fonts feel harmonious in body text
  - Character width rhythm should create compatible texture when both fonts are set at the same size
  - "You are a casting director for a movie...seeking typographic actors to fill roles...Be sure that the overall personalities of your actors do not clash."
- Pairings extracted: none (article is principle-focused, no named pairings)
